The core problem
Budget = headcount. There is no other lever.

To increase throughput, we have to add people. There is no way to parallelize work, no AI layer to absorb investigation and generation tasks, and no automated gates to replace manual handoffs.

Client budget goes to coordination overhead, translation loops, and wait time instead of meaningful outcomes.

What this looks like in practice
A well-scoped ticket still waits in 3 separate queues

Intake → backlog → SA queue (wait) → SA review → AC queue (wait) → AC creation → sprint planning → dev allocation (wait) → development → manual review → manual testing → manual deploy

Every "wait" is a shared-resource gate. None of them are automated.

Talking point: We are not failing because people aren't working hard. We are constrained by a model that has no AI layer. The process was designed for a world where humans do everything.
Slide 3 of 8 — Where Time Goes
The 3 bottlenecks
These are the points in the current process where budget is spent waiting, not building.
Bottleneck 1
SA Feasibility Queue

SA bandwidth is shared across all active accounts. Tickets sit waiting for an available slot.

When SA picks up the ticket, they start from zero: read the codebase manually, ask questions, wait for answers, re-review.

Simple requests: 10-15 minutes. Complex: up to 1 week. The queue wait adds on top of that.

Bottleneck 2
AC Creation Queue

After SA sign-off, the ticket enters a second queue: waiting for QA to be allocated to write acceptance criteria.

QA writes AC from SA-reviewed requirements. Gaps in those requirements open another loop back to the PDM and client.

Accumulates on top of the SA wait. Budget constraints mean no dedicated QA per account.

Bottleneck 3
Manual Code Review

2-3 hours per PR. The reviewer must manually rebuild the business context from the Zoho ticket, read all changed files, and verify against AC.

No automated diff summary. No pre-analysis. Senior dev starts from scratch every time.

Delays the merge-to-staging cycle and pulls senior attention away from active work.

Talking point: All three bottlenecks are solved by the same thing: AI taking over the investigation and generation work, so humans are approving and governing instead of producing from scratch.
Slide 4 of 8 — AI-Native SDLC
What AI-native actually means
Same roles. Same clients. Fundamentally different distribution of work between humans and AI.
Principle 1
AI investigates. Humans approve.

AI runs feasibility checks against the codebase in minutes. AI generates the Architecture Decision Record. SA reviews and approves a pre-analyzed plan instead of investigating from scratch.

SA role shifts from bottleneck investigator (days) to architectural approver (minutes).

Principle 2
Parallel execution replaces sequential queues.

Claude works across 3-4 parallel git worktrees simultaneously. Three to four features build at once with no merge conflicts.

QA builds automation test scripts during development, not after. CICD runs tests during queue time that used to be dead time.

Principle 3
Requirements live in the codebase.

ADRs (Architecture Decision Records) are generated by AI and committed to the repo. They are always in sync with the code.

No separate requirements doc to track. No "the ticket said X but the code does Y." The ADR is the single source of truth.

Principle 4
Automated gates replace manual handoffs.

PR gates run automatically on every commit: unit tests, linting, build checks. No manual trigger needed.

Production deploy triggers when all gates are green and human approvals are in. No manual release steps.

Talking point: The 11 phases do not change. What changes is who does the work at each phase: AI for investigation and generation, humans for judgment and approval.
Slide 5 of 8 — Role Shifts
How each role changes
Nobody disappears. The nature of the work shifts from production to governance.
PM / PDM
Manual intake routing, budget bucket checks, translation loops between client, SA, and dev throughout entire cycle
→
Prompts Claude with context. Reviews AI-generated requirements. Sends ADR to client for formal sign-off before dev starts.
SA
Reads codebase from scratch on every ticket. Answers back-and-forth clarification questions. Investigation takes up to 1 week.
→
Reviews AI-pre-analyzed feasibility. Approves ADR in minutes. On standby for architectural judgment -- not routine investigation.
Developer
Writes code manually, one task at a time. No AI assistance. Context-switches back to finished work when QA finds bugs.
→
Directs Claude across 3-4 parallel worktrees. Reviews plans before AI executes. Orchestrator, not code author.
QA
Sole manual quality gate. No automation, no device catalog, no integration test coverage. Writes AC scenarios after SA review -- from vague requirements.
→
Validates AC during ADR generation. Builds automation scripts in parallel with dev. Focuses real-device testing on what AI cannot simulate.
DM
Manually tracks resource availability across all accounts. Reactively assigns SA, QA, and dev when capacity opens. Commits to delivery timelines based on informal estimates with no automated tracking.
→
Governs strategic capacity and prioritization. AI handles tactical routing and budget bucket tracking. Delivery commitments are backed by gate data and real throughput metrics, not gut feel.
Client
Submits request and disappears until delivery. Often unclear what shipped or what to validate at release.
→
Approves structured ADR before dev starts. Reviews prototype. Formal sign-off at release confirms what they approved was what shipped.
Talking point: This is not about replacing roles. It is about moving each role out of the reactive loop and into a governing position. Every role becomes more strategic, not less necessary.
